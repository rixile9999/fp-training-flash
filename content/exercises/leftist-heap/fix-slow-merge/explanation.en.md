The original `make` attached the two children to the left and right exactly in the order it received them. `merge` always recurses toward the right child, so with this arrangement the merge results keep piling up on the right. When you insert in ascending order, each new value is attached at the end of the right branch, so the branch grows to length n, one insert becomes O(n), and the whole run O(n^2). Values still come out in the right order, so the bug hardly shows if you only look at test results.

There is only one place to fix: `make`. Compare the ranks of the two children, put the larger one on the left, and set the node's rank to the rank of the (smaller) child that ends up on the right, plus 1. Then at every node the right branch is the shortest branch, and since a heap of rank r has at least 2^r - 1 nodes, the right branch is limited to O(log n). All the other operations build nodes with `make`, so keeping the invariant in that one place fixes everything (`persistent-data-structures`).

There are two common half-fixes. If you swap the children but compute the rank from the left (larger) side, the node stores a rank that differs from the definition. Even if ascending input does not slow down right away, later placements of children are based on the wrong rank, and the guarantee that the right branch is short disappears. If you always swap the two children without comparing, you break the cases where the left side was already larger.
